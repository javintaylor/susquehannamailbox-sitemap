DRIVERS AND EVIDENCE BEHIND THE SIX INDICATORS
Research record for the Maternal Care Task Force, Cecil and Harford Counties
Compiled 2026-09-04

WHAT THIS IS
The Task Force measures itself against six indicators. This document records
what the research says causes each one, what the national, Maryland and local
data show, which data system would reveal each driver at county level, and
which communities have measurably improved comparable numbers. It is the
source behind the Drivers and Case Studies tabs on the member site.

METHOD AND LIMITATION
Three parallel research passes: national etiology, Maryland and local drivers,
and case studies. Roughly 230 searches in total. Direct page retrieval was
blocked for nearly every source domain, so figures come from search-index
extracts of the cited pages rather than from full-text reads. Every number
below appeared in an extract of the page cited. Items that could not be
confirmed are flagged as gaps rather than smoothed over. Before any figure
goes on a slide for an external audience, open the primary source.

======================================================================
THE HEADLINE FINDING
======================================================================
Read together, the six indicators carry diagnostic information that no single
indicator carries alone.

Cecil's very low birth weight rate (1.9%) is far above Harford (1.2%) and
Maryland (1.5%), while its total low birth weight rate (9.2%) is barely above
the state (8.9%). At the same time Cecil's prenatal care entry is BETTER than
the state (72.6% versus 69.3%) and its no-care rate is LOWER (4.5% versus
7.1%).

That combination points at extreme prematurity and where those infants are
delivered, not at late prenatal care and not at term growth restriction.

Two consequences for strategy:
 1. Prenatal care initiation should be treated as a targeting tool for finding
    a small high-risk group, not as the headline problem. Cecil already beats
    the state on both care measures.
 2. The highest-value question is where Cecil's very low birth weight infants
    are now being delivered, because mortality is 62% higher when such an
    infant is born outside a Level III centre.

DRIVERS BY INDICATOR
======================================================================
Full detail with sources is in the Drivers tab on the site (17 rows). Summary:

INFANT MORTALITY (Cecil 7.6 / Harford 4.4 / Maryland 5.5 per 1,000)
 - Prematurity is the engine. Short gestation and low birthweight cause 14.5%
   of US infant deaths and are the leading cause of neonatal death in
   Maryland. About 54% of infant deaths occur among the 2% born before 32
   weeks.
 - The rural excess is postneonatal. Rural infant mortality runs 27% above
   large metro, but neonatal is only 15% higher while POSTNEONATAL is 50%
   higher. Postneonatal deaths are dominated by sudden unexpected infant death
   and injury, and about half are considered preventable.
 - Smoking drives sudden unexpected infant death. Adjusted odds ratio 2.44;
   eliminating prenatal smoking would prevent roughly 22% of such deaths.
   Maryland Child Fatality Review found 60% bed-sharing and 68% of families
   HAD a crib available, so the gap is behaviour and counselling, not supply
   alone.

PRETERM BIRTH (12.1 / 10.1 / 10.5%)
 - Prior preterm birth (relative risk 5.64) and interpregnancy interval under
   6 months (adjusted odds ratio 2.2 for extremely preterm). Both identifiable
   at the first visit; both have established responses.
 - Substance use. Cannabis raises preterm odds 1.52. For opioid use disorder,
   continuous medication treatment lowers preterm odds to 0.6 and low birth
   weight to 0.4 versus untreated, so treatment CONTINUITY is the variable.
 - Losing the obstetric unit. Measured, not theoretical: +0.67 percentage
   points preterm in rural non-adjacent counties the year after closure, and
   24% higher odds of inadequate prenatal care (38% among Medicaid) in Iowa.
 - Chronic conditions before pregnancy. Among US preterm births, 34.8% of
   mothers had unhealthy weight and 10.4% hypertension in pregnancy.

LOW AND VERY LOW BIRTH WEIGHT (9.2 / 9.0 / 8.9% and 1.9 / 1.2 / 1.5%)
 - Smoking is the largest modifiable cause of restricted growth, with the
   highest population attributable fraction of any behavioural factor.
 - Delivery location decides very low birth weight survival. 62% higher odds
   of death outside a Level III hospital; hospitals under 50 such deliveries a
   year show 15-36% higher odds of death or severe haemorrhage; regionalization
   modelling suggests 21% of these deaths are preventable.

EARLY AND NO PRENATAL CARE (72.6 / 78.7 / 69.3% and 4.5 / 3.2 / 7.1%)
 - Documented reasons for late care are operational, not attitudinal: did not
   know she was pregnant (33-42%), could not get an appointment (25-36%), the
   practice would not start earlier (28%), money or insurance (25-31%), no
   Medicaid card (19-36%).
 - Measure ADEQUACY, not just the first visit. The Kotelchuck index separates
   a woman who booked early then stopped attending from one who completed care.
   After a closure the failure mode is attendance across the pregnancy.
 - Cecil's no-care group is roughly 50 women a year. That is small enough to
   characterise individually rather than address with a campaign. Fear of being
   identified as a substance user is documented in 73.3% of pregnant women with
   substance use disorder.

CROSS-CUTTING
 - Perinatal mental health. Maryland PRAMS: 15.6% postpartum depressive
   symptoms; 91% attended a postpartum visit but 35% never discussed
   depression with a provider. Behavioural health is the leading cause of
   pregnancy-associated death in Maryland.
 - Material hardship. Threatened eviction relative risk 1.73 for low birth
   weight or preterm; food insecurity odds ratio 1.25 for low birth weight;
   64% of Maryland sudden unexpected infant deaths were in high-poverty ZIPs.

======================================================================
LOCAL DRIVERS WITH EVIDENCE
======================================================================
CECIL
 - Overdose mortality 82.0 per 100,000 (2023), second highest in Maryland
   behind Baltimore City and about double the state rate of 41.8. A 2025 CDC
   Epi-Aid found medetomidine in 48.8% of Cecil drug-check samples against
   8.2% in Harford. This is the best-documented local risk factor available.
 - About 70% of Union Hospital deliveries were Maryland Medicaid; Medicaid
   births carry higher preterm rates nationally (11.7% versus 9.6%).
 - Roughly 1,123 births in 2024, about one third high risk; Union delivered
   661 of them. The Family Birth Center closed 2026-06-30.
 - Nearest Level III neonatal intensive care is Christiana Hospital in Newark,
   Delaware, about 13 miles. UM Upper Chesapeake is about 40 miles and Level II.
 - Ranked in the bottom quartile of Maryland counties on health outcomes (2021).

HARFORD
 - Adult smoking 16% against 13% statewide.
 - Below its own Community Health Improvement Plan target of 80% first
   trimester prenatal care, at 78.7%.
 - CHIP targets already adopted: infant mortality at or below 4.0, low birth
   weight at or below 6.1%, first trimester care at or above 80%, and
   substance-exposed newborns at or below 30.0 per 1,000.
 - Lowest educational attainment and highest poverty concentrate in the
   Aberdeen and Edgewood corridor. Within-county disparity data was not found.

DATA GAPS THE TASK FORCE SHOULD FILL
======================================================================
These are the specific things nobody could verify, in priority order.

1. Cecil and Harford infant deaths 2019-2023 split by neonatal versus
   postneonatal and by cause. This single pull decides the whole strategy.
2. Where Cecil residents now give birth. The facility-of-birth field on the
   birth certificate, including Delaware and Pennsylvania facilities, for the
   second half of 2026 against prior years.
3. Very low birth weight births by hospital of delivery and whether that
   hospital is Level III or higher. This is Title V national performance
   measure 3 and the most defensible outcome metric available.
4. Maternal smoking rate on birth certificates for both counties. Decides
   whether cessation is the top intervention.
5. County neonatal abstinence and substance-exposed newborn rates, from HSCRC
   newborn discharge data and Department of Human Services reports.
6. Prenatal care adequacy (Kotelchuck) distribution by county and payer, not
   just first-trimester entry.
7. Which HealthChoice managed care organizations Christiana Hospital accepts,
   and when the six-month free transport arrangement ends. Most consequential
   unresolved question, and it has a deadline.
8. Whether a Fetal and Infant Mortality Review team can be established for
   Cecil. Identified Maryland teams are Howard, Montgomery, Baltimore City,
   Charles and Wicomico. Neither Cecil nor Harford appeared, unconfirmed.
9. Cecil and Harford sleep-related infant death case summaries from Child
   Fatality Review.
10. March of Dimes 2026 maternity care desert designation for both counties.
    Cecil now has zero birthing facilities, so its designation should change.

RECOMMENDED FIRST STEP
One consolidated data request to MDH Vital Statistics covering items 1-6 for
both counties, submitted jointly by the two health departments. Everything
else on the list is a phone call or a letter.

CASE STUDIES: WHAT HAS ACTUALLY WORKED
======================================================================
24 case studies are on the site with before and after numbers, evidence
quality, funding and a transferability note. Grouped by what they support:

STRONGEST EVIDENCE, AND RUNNABLE WITHOUT A HOSPITAL
 - CenteringPregnancy group prenatal care. Randomised trial: preterm 9.8%
   versus 13.8%. South Carolina Medicaid: 36% lower preterm, 44% lower low
   birth weight, positive return on investment. Can run in a federally
   qualified health centre.
 - Baby and Me Tobacco Free. Low birth weight 4.9% for completers versus
   11.6%. Runs out of a health department or WIC office. Cheapest item here.
 - Progesterone and aspirin screening at every visit (Ohio). Births before 32
   weeks down 20.5% among women with a prior preterm birth.
 - Medicaid-billed paraprofessional home visiting. Kentucky low birth weight
   odds ratio 0.54; Wisconsin 17% lower preterm and reduced infant mortality.
 - Telemedicine maternal-fetal medicine and regionalization (Arkansas).
   Very low birth weight deliveries at non-NICU hospitals fell 13.1% to 7.0%;
   statewide infant mortality 8.5 to 7.0.
 - Perinatal substance use integration (West Virginia, Kentucky). Birthweight
   +208 grams and preterm odds ratio 0.56; neonatal intensive care admission
   39.1% versus 48.8%.

MOST TRANSFERABLE BY INSTITUTIONAL FIT
 - B'more for Healthy Babies. Same state, same Title V agency. Infant
   mortality down 35% over a decade; sleep-related deaths roughly halved.
 - Babies Born Healthy sites. Wicomico down 22.4% and Prince George's down
   26.8%, while Somerset with no site rose 31.2%. Cecil is not a site. This is
   the cleanest state-level ask available.
 - Delaware Healthy Mother and Infant Consortium. Infant mortality 9.3 to 5.9.
   Cecil families now deliver inside this system, which makes a cross-border
   arrangement concrete rather than aspirational.
 - HRSA Rural Maternity and Obstetrics Management Strategies. The closest
   federal template for a county that just lost labour and delivery.

CAUTIONARY, INCLUDED DELIBERATELY
 - Nurse-Family Partnership. Three randomised trials, largely null for birth
   outcomes. Do not present it as a preterm birth reducer.
 - Rideshare transportation. Randomised trial found no change in prenatal care
   adequacy or preterm delivery. Transport matters, but rides alone do not fix
   attendance.
 - Michigan safe sleep education mandate. Twelve years, no significant change.
   Education without saturation does not move deaths.
 - Alabama Infant Mortality Reduction Initiative. Statewide rate improved, but
   infant mortality fell 39.8% among white married mothers and ROSE 54.7%
   among Black married mothers. A targeted bundle can widen gaps. Pre-specify
   equity metrics before launching anything.
