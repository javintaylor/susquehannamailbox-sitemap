PERINATAL CARE PROGRAMS AND RESOURCES — CECIL AND HARFORD COUNTIES, MARYLAND
Research report for the Maternal Care Task Force member site
Compiled 2026-09-03. All web facts come from search-indexed program pages
and news coverage; direct page fetches were blocked in the research
environment, so confidence ratings are given per item. Items marked
"member report" come from the 2026-09-03 Regional System of Care Action
Team kickoff and were not independently verified online.

======================================================================
KEY FINDINGS
======================================================================
1. Cecil County has had no in-county labor and delivery since 2026-06-30,
   when ChristianaCare closed Union Hospital's 11-bed Family Birth Center
   (fewer than 0.5 deliveries/day; 31 positions cut). Union's ED still
   evaluates and treats active labor and may deliver before transfer.
   ChristianaCare offered free transport to Christiana Hospital (Newark,
   DE, about 13 miles) for six months and Christiana reportedly accepts
   Maryland Medicaid "for the time being." The Maryland Health Care
   Commission held a public hearing 2026-06-11.
   >> Most important item to verify: which HealthChoice MCOs Christiana
      Hospital contracts with and what happens after ~December 2026.

2. Federal money is flowing but no Cecil/Harford maternity line item is
   confirmed. Maryland received $168.2M for RHTP Budget Period 1; MDH
   announced $80M in first-round subawards to 41 recipients effective
   2026-08-10. The recipient list could not be retrieved. Cecil and
   Harford are RHTP-eligible rural jurisdictions but not among the nine
   federally designated priority counties.

3. Not currently serving the region: HRSA Healthy Start (Baltimore City
   only), RMOMS (Lower Shore only), Babies Born Healthy (no Cecil or
   Harford site verified), MIECHV-funded home visiting (Baltimore City and
   Cambridge; MDH is assessing expansion). Maryland is NOT in the CMS
   Transforming Maternal Health model.

4. 2026 Maryland laws relevant to the Task Force:
   - SB 169 (Ch. 788): hospital duty to examine/stabilize emergency
     pregnancy-related conditions or transfer; effective 2026-10-01.
   - HB 1118 / SB 891: insurers and Medicaid must cover standardized
     perinatal behavioral health screening through 12 months postpartum.
   - HB 158: Maternal Health Monitoring Pilot (remote monitoring for
     hypertension/diabetes, up to 150 Medicaid enrollees/yr); enactment
     status not confirmed.
   - No 2026 bill found on birth-center licensure, doula rates, or
     advance notice of L&D closures (Maine passed a 120-day notice law).

5. Medicaid: full coverage through 12 months postpartum since 2022-04-01;
   pregnancy eligibility to about 264% FPL; Healthy Babies Equity Act
   covers pregnancy regardless of immigration status; doula benefit with
   self-referral extended through 2026-12-31.

6. Data: latest published Vital Statistics reports are 2023 (the Task
   Force slides cite 2024 VSA figures). CDC PRAMS collection was
   interrupted in January 2025. March of Dimes released its 2026
   Maternity Care Deserts report 2026-08-11; Cecil's designation should
   be re-checked now that the closure is in effect.

FEDERAL PROGRAMS
======================================================================
Rural Health Transformation Program (CMS) — Both counties eligible
  Maryland competitive solicitation: "Rooted and Resilient Rural Maryland:
  Transformation through Sustainable Access, Innovative Care, and Tech
  Innovation" (PHPA 3936). Cecil applied with Union Hospital and DES.
  https://www.highergov.com/sl/contract-opportunity/md-phpa-3936-rooted-and-resilient-rural-m-66259458/
  $50B/5-year program (2025 reconciliation law). Maryland BP1 award
  $168,180,838 (plan approved 2025-12-29). $80M first-round subawards
  (41 recipients, effective 2026-08-10) in three focus areas: rural
  workforce; sustainable access and innovative care (regional mobile
  health / MIH, community care teams); Eat for Health.
  https://health.maryland.gov/pophealth/Pages/Rural-Health-Transformation-Program.aspx
  https://marylandmatters.org/2026/08/11/medical-systems-local-health-agencies-get-80-million-in-federal-funds-to-boost-rural-health/
  Confidence: High (totals/dates). Recipient list not retrieved.

Title V MCH Block Grant — Statewide
  FY24 federal award $12,377,354 plus state match. Cecil and Harford among
  13 LHDs using core funds for perinatal care coordination/home visiting.
  https://health.maryland.gov/phpa/mch/  |  https://mchb.tvisdata.hrsa.gov/
  Confidence: High.

Healthy Start (HRSA) — Not in region
  Maryland grantee: Baltimore Healthy Start, Inc. https://baltimorehealthystart.org/
  Confidence: Medium.

MIECHV Home Visiting — Statewide
  MDH-administered (FY23 $10.4M); funds NFP and Healthy Families in
  Baltimore City and an HFA expansion in Cambridge; readiness-assessment
  RFA to expand. https://health.maryland.gov/phpa/mch/pages/home_visiting.aspx
  Confidence: Medium. FY26 award and Cecil/Harford funding source unconfirmed.
